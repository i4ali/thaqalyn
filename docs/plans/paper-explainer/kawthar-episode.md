# Paper explainer - Episode 2: "Ten words against one insult" (al-Kawthar)

Format: papercraft-diorama explainer (Paper Planet mechanics: one sentence = one shot = one
literal prop; dry single voice; no music; page-to-world opener; small all-caps captions). Tone:
wonder. Sacred figures are never shown: no Prophet, no Ali, no Fatima. The mocker (al-As ibn
Wa'il) and the Quraysh elders may appear as faceless paper silhouettes; the household is always
an object (a river, a cup, a seedling, a tree).

Target: 10 shots, ~95 s, ~235 words. Slightly over the 60-90 s default on purpose: the arc needs
the taunt, the gift, the verdict, and the re-read, and the shortest surah in the Qur'an deserves
all three of its verses on screen. No end card, no CTA.

Why this for social: a ten-word surah that answers a playground-grade insult ("cut off") with a
gift, a river, and a reversal, and the twist lands on a daughter. Hook is a number and a fight.

Sources: everything is lifted from the audited `docs/plans/surah-experience/kawthar-script.md`
(four-auditor audit 2026-07-28; sourcing notes there): occasion of revelation and "his name dies
with him" (Majma al-Bayan; sons Qasim then Abdullah from al-Mizan); a'tayna as already-given
ownership (al-Mizan); the river narration and "for me, for you, and for those who love you after
me" (Nur al-Thaqalayn 5/682, Amali al-Tusi 69); verse 3 as reversal-restriction with al-As ibn
Wa'il as the shani' (al-Mizan); the progeny argument (al-Mizan, verbatim confirmed); Fakhr
al-Razi's "see how many of them have been killed, yet the world is full of them" (al-Tafsir
al-Kabir on 108:1). Word count of the surah (10) verified by hand on quran_data.json.
Respectful-language rule: the mocker's sneer is rendered "when he is gone", never "when he dies".

---

## Voiceover (one line per shot)

1. The shortest surah in the Qur'an is ten words long, and it came down to answer one insult.
2. In Mecca, a man with no surviving sons was called abtar. Cut off. A stump. A line that ends.
3. The Prophet had buried two infant sons, Qasim, then Abdullah, and a chief of Quraysh saw his opening.
4. Asked who he had been talking to, al-As ibn Wa'il said: that abtar. When he is gone, his name goes with him.
5. Heaven's reply does not defend him. It opens with a gift: Truly, We have given you al-Kawthar. Abundance, already given.
6. He told his companions of a river in Paradise, whiter than milk, sweeter than honey. And to Ali: this river is for me, for you, and for those who love you after me.
7. One thing is asked back: so pray to your Lord, and offer.
8. Then four words end it: the one who hates you, he is the cut-off one. The insult is lifted off him and set down on the man who said it.
9. Now read the gift again. They mocked the sons he had buried, so the abundance had to hold what they said he lacked. And at its center stood not a son but a daughter.
10. Through Fatima came a line so vast that a Sunni commentator, Fakhr al-Razi, stopped here to marvel: see how many of them have been killed, and the world is still full of them.

Word count: ~235.

## Style block (locked, prepended to every still prompt)

Handmade papercraft diorama photographed with a macro lens, tilt-shift shallow depth of field,
soft warm studio light. Everything is cut from layered cream, sand and kraft card with visible
paper fibre and tiny cast shadows at every layer edge. Any figures are simple low-poly paper
silhouettes with no faces. Accent palette: deep emerald and gold leaf. Vertical composition,
dark warm bokeh background.

Reference: `assets/style_ref_book.jpg` with `--ref` on shots 1 and 10. Continuity: shot 2's
still passed as `--ref` to shot 9; shot 4's still passed as `--ref` to shot 8.
