Yaqin - A Descent Through Three Depths
Consolidated Audit Findings

Retroactive audit of the first shipped theme dive, per the theme-deep-dive skill: three flag-only auditors - A (flow, shape & the ledger), B (theology, sourcing & Arabic), C (readability). Yaqin predates the design-doc convention, so every source was verified from scratch and the ledger check ran in reverse (did later dives reuse Yaqin material?). Overlapping findings are merged; every item names its auditor(s). Nothing has been changed - each finding awaits your approval.

The Qur'anic layer is fully verified (8/8) and the ledger is clean - but the dive's only narration is a misattributed composite of two reports, and much of the prose predates the “surface of the text” readability bar the later dives were held to.

Blockers

2 findings

One sourcing blocker on the dive's only narration, and one first-pass misparse that briefly reads God as the smoke - on the dive's flagship metaphor.

1
“The Morning of Ashura” is a misattributed composite - and cites no book
BlockerBeat 12 · NarrationAuditors A + B + C

The beat welds two different reports into one, under the wrong single narrator at the wrong time of day. Hilal ibn Nafi's genuine report is at the Imam's last breath, not the morning: standing in Umar ibn Sa'd's ranks he said he had never seen a slain man drenched in his blood “more handsome or more luminous of face” (Maqtal al-Husayn of al-Muqarram; quoted in Bihar al-Anwar) - it says nothing about arrows falling thicker. The “as the trial intensified, his face grew brighter and calmer” report is narrated from Imam Ali ibn al-Husayn (Ma'ani al-Akhbar of al-Saduq / Bihar al-Anwar). The source line - Radiance at Karbala - narrated of Hilal ibn Nafi - names no book at all: “Radiance at Karbala” is an invented display title, and “narrated of” is non-idiomatic besides. WikiShia also flags a known name-inversion confusion between Nafi' ibn Hilal (a martyred companion) and “Hilal ibn Nafi'” of the opposing army, which makes a bare-name citation weaker still. Every other dive in the series names a real collection.

Fix options: (a) re-anchor the body to Hilal's genuine death-scene report - even the enemy soldier saw the light - retag away from “The Morning of Ashura”, and cite Maqtal al-Husayn of al-Muqarram · Bihar al-Anwar; or (b) keep the intensification framing, retitle (e.g. “When the Trial Grew Fierce”), re-attribute to Imam Ali ibn al-Husayn, drop the arrows detail, and cite Ma'ani al-Akhbar · Bihar al-Anwar. Note option (b) makes the dua beat's author the narrator of the summit scene - a weld Yaqin already plays (“present at Karbala”), which would strengthen it further.

2
The flagship metaphor's first application parses as “God is the smoke”
BlockerBeat 06 · 6:76 reflectionAuditor C

The colon appositive grammatically attaches to the nearest noun phrase - “the One who never does” - so the first-pass parse makes God the smoke: a reading that is wrong and momentarily blasphemy-adjacent, at the first real application of the dive's central image. The intended referent (the setting stars as Depth I's inferential evidence) is nowhere on the sentence's surface. Secondary: the inner quote “that he might be of those of certainty” is 6:75 - a verse not shown on this screen and not cited, so it floats. Ranked Blocker on Auditor C's judgment: a theologically wrong first-pass reading, not mere elevation.

CurrentStar, moon, sun - each one sets, until Ibrahim turns his face to the One who never does: the smoke on the horizon that proves the fire.
SuggestedStar, moon, sun - each one sets, until Ibrahim turns his face to the One who never sets. The setting stars were his smoke on the horizon: proof of a fire he had not yet seen.

Should-fix

15 findings

Two structural (the Mother of Musa beat and the flame hinge), one narrative gap on the summit approach, and a cluster of unglossed references - the exact al-Duha pattern the later audits were tightened against. Yaqin fixes must be mirrored into the UR/AR strings.

3
“After the sons.” - the deaths are never stated anywhere in the dive
Should-fixBeat 13 · The CourtAuditor C · borderline Blocker

“After the sons” elides a predicate (“were killed”) that English ellipsis cannot recover forward - and no beat ever says the Imam and his men were killed: beat 12 shows only arrows and a luminous face. A first-pass reader reaches “After the sons.” with no on-screen event to attach it to; the third fragment finally carries a verb and repairs the pattern retroactively - which is precisely a reread. “The tyrant” resolves only against the source caption.

CurrentAfter the sons. After the brothers. After the tents burned and the caravan was driven in chains - she stood in the court of the tyrant and said she had witnessed nothing but beauty.
SuggestedAfter her sons were killed. After her brothers. After the tents burned and the caravan was driven to Kufa in chains - she stood in the tyrant's court and said she had seen nothing but beauty.
4
The Mother of Musa does Movement III's work inside Movement II
Should-fixBeat 10 · 28:7Auditors A + C

Movement II is “The Witnessing” - but nothing is seen in this beat: she casts her infant on the water sight-unseen, on God's word alone. The reflection then lands Movement III's turn a movement early (“certainty is no longer a thought. It has become an act” is the “live it” language the Act III card is about to claim as its reveal), so the summit's step-change is dulled. She is also not a prophet, yet the Act III connector says You have seen it in the prophets.

Direction: re-point the beat at the seeing that seals her story - 28:13, the child returned “that she might know that the promise of God is true” (she trusted first, then her eyes confirmed) - or retire the “act” sentence and let the beat be about trusting the unseen word. Align the Act III connector either way (e.g. “You have seen it in those God chose.”).

5
Depth 3's image is enacted in Depth 2, and the hinge is never drawn
Should-fixBeats 02b · 09 · 11Auditor A

The threshold map defines Haqq al-Yaqin as “To stand within the flame itself” - and two beats later Ibrahim is literally standing within a flame, alive, filed under “witnessing.” The Act III card's differentiator (“was asked to stand inside the flame - while still alive”) is also literally true of Ibrahim, so it fails to distinguish. The intended hinge - Ibrahim's fire was commanded cool, the family's fire at Karbala was not - is never said, so the ladder wobbles at its central image.

Direction: one clause, in the beat 09 reflection or on the Act III card, naming the hinge: for Ibrahim the flame was made cool - he witnessed; for the family the flame was not cooled - they lived it. That single distinction repairs the whole ladder.

6
Beat 12's surface assumes the whole story: who, where, what day
Should-fixBeat 12 · body + reflectionAuditor CPairs with #1

“Upon them” has no antecedent on the screen; “They said” opens with a second unanchored pronoun; Husayn is never identified as the family of beat 11 or the Prophet's ﷺ grandson; the day called Ashura is never placed at Karbala. In the reflection, “the Beloved” and “the meeting” are unglossed euphemisms (God; death) a first-pass reader does not get from the surface - the exact al-Duha pattern. Whichever fix option #1 takes, the rewrite should carry these identifications in-text (e.g. “as the arrows fell thicker on Husayn - the Prophet's ﷺ grandson - and his companions…”; “The closer he drew to meeting his Lord, the brighter the certainty grew.”).

7
The Court's source line names no book - and identifies no one
Should-fixBeat 13 · sourceAuditors B + C

The content itself verified fully (see clean areas) - but Sayyida Zaynab, in the court of Ibn Ziyad - Kufa cites no book (the report's earliest appearance is al-Luhuf of Ibn Tawus; also Bihar al-Anwar 45:115-116), and neither person is identified: Zaynab's relation to Husayn and Ibn Ziyad's role are both assumed, so the body's “the tyrant” resolves only for a reader who already knows. Suggested: “Sayyida Zaynab, sister of Husayn, in the court of Ibn Ziyad - al-Luhuf of Ibn Tawus” (mirrored in UR/AR).

8
The three-depths frame is announced three times before the first story
Should-fixBeats 02 · 02b · 04Auditor A

The orientation promises “to know it, to see it, to live it”; the depths map details the same three; then beat 04's reflection announces it a third time: “The Qur'an says it arrives in depths - three of them.” Since Movement I holds only two beats and one is spent re-stating the map, the first movement effectively contains a single narrative beat - five screens pass before anything happens. Direction: keep 102:5 (it is where the term lives) but retask its reflection to Ilm-specific work - al-Takathur's own scene: the rivalry that distracts “until you visit the graves”; knowing before seeing.

9
“The Question” has no question under it, and the conditional dangles
Should-fixBeat 04 · 102:5Auditors A + C

102:5 is a rebuke-conditional - nothing on the screen asks anything, so the tag and the text point in different directions on the dive's first content screen. And “if only you knew…” is left hanging: the apodosis (102:6-7 - you would surely see the Blaze… then see it with the eye of certainty, the very verse the map cites for Depth 2) is never delivered anywhere in the dive. Direction: retag (e.g. “The Naming” / “The First Depth”) or let the reflection close the conditional - knowledge first, then the eye - which also quietly justifies the map's 102:7 reference.

10
2:260's excerpt hides both the asker and the ask
Should-fixBeat 08 · translationAuditor C

The first speaker is never named (it is God), and “but so that my heart may be at rest” answers a request cut from the excerpt (show me how You give life to the dead) - so “so that” hangs: so that what may happen? The tag supplies “asks to see” but not what he asked to see. Direction: a bracketed lead-in, e.g. [Ibrahim said: “My Lord, show me how You give life to the dead.”] God said: “Do you not believe?” He said: “Yes - but so that my heart may be at rest.”

11
Garden path on the Movement II card: “those God chose to merely believe”
Should-fixBeat 07 · Act II cardAuditor C

First-pass parse attaches “to merely believe” to “chose” - people whom God chose for the purpose of merely believing - and the semicolon clause forces a re-parse.

CurrentThe Qur'an did not leave the prophets or those God chose to merely believe; it let them witness, with their own eyes.
SuggestedThe Qur'an did not leave the prophets, or the ones God chose, with belief alone; it let them witness with their own eyes.
12
“The Friend of God” unglossed, and the depths unnamed at the hinge
Should-fixBeat 08 · reflectionAuditor C

“The Friend of God” requires knowing Khalilullah to link epithet to the Ibrahim named in the tag; and “the first depth to the second” names neither depth on this screen (the names live on the map and movement cards).

CurrentEven the Friend of God, who already believed, longed to witness. He is asking to move from the first depth to the second.
SuggestedEven Ibrahim - the Friend of God - who already believed, longed to witness. He is asking to move from the first depth to the second: from knowing to seeing.
13
Beat 09: a fire with no event, credited to the wrong cause
Should-fixBeat 09 · 21:69Auditors A + C

Two stumbles on one screen. (a) “Cast into the flames” arrives with no agent and no event - nothing prior says his people threw him into a fire, and a reader who just learned “fire” as the dive's metaphor may briefly read this fire figuratively too. (b) The verse shows God commanding (“We said: O fire - be coolness”) while the reflection says “the fire itself submits to his certainty” - screen and reflection assign the cause differently, a slippage visible in a single glance. Direction: “His people cast him into a fire for breaking their idols - and there, at his Lord's word, the fire submits: coolness and peace for the one who was certain of Him.”

14
“Ahl al-Bayt” unglossed on the dive's very first line
Should-fixBeat 01 · openAuditor C

“A descent through the Qur'an and the Ahl al-Bayt - in three depths” leads with a transliterated term whose plain-English identification does not arrive until beat 11 (and reads oddly literally - descending “through” people). This is the app's core vocabulary, which may be reason to accept it - but per the audit bar it is ranked, not footnoted. Options: “…the Qur'an and the Prophet's ﷺ household”, or keep and gloss: “…and the Ahl al-Bayt, the Prophet's ﷺ household - in three depths.”

15
The dua cites no number
Should-fixBeat 15 · sourceAuditors A + B

The Arabic verified word-for-word as the opening of Sahifa Dua 20 (Makarim al-Akhlaq), fairly omitting the opening salawat - but Imam Ali ibn al-Husayn · al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya gives no dua number, so a 54-dua compilation citation is not locatable by the reader. Tawakkul and Shukr cite “Dua 28” / “Dua 37”. Suggested: al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya, Dua 20 (Makarim al-Akhlaq) in all three languages.

16
Leading ayn marks break the house rule - with two different characters
Should-fixActInfo + depths mapAuditor B

House rule: ayn becomes a straight apostrophe only between two letters, otherwise dropped - word-initial ayn in “Ilm”/“Ayn” takes no mark. The file has it both ways: ActInfo tr: "'Ilm al-Yaqin" / "'Ayn al-Yaqin" (straight apostrophe U+0027) and depths items "‘Ilm al-Yaqin" / "‘Ayn al-Yaqin" (curly U+2018) - a violation twice over and internally inconsistent (“Haqq al-Yaqin” is clean in both sites). All four become “Ilm al-Yaqin” / “Ayn al-Yaqin”.

17
Missing ﷺ on the Prophet's one English mention
Should-fixBeat 11 · Act III cardAuditor B

“But one family, the Prophet's own household, was asked to stand inside the flame” - no ﷺ in English, while the Urdu (رسول اللہﷺ) and Arabic (صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم) both carry the honorific. Suggested: “the Prophet's ﷺ own household” or “the household of the Prophet ﷺ”.

Polish

9 findings

18
The closing prompt asks two different questions
PolishBeat 14 · ReturnAuditors A + C

The prompt (“Where is your yaqin?”) asks the reader to locate existing certainty; the subline asks them to “name the certainty you long for” - a certainty they lack. The placeholder rescues it, but Sabr and Tawakkul's prompts are single-minded. Suggested: align on the subline's intent - “What do you long to be certain of?” (or “Where do you need yaqin?”).

19
Bare “Husayn” without “Imam” twice in English
PolishBeats 12 + 15Auditor B

“the face of Husayn only grew more luminous” (beat 12) and “The son of Husayn” (beat 15 note) drop the title the parallel Urdu carries (امام حسینؑ) and the file itself uses elsewhere (“Imam Ali ibn al-Husayn”). Suggested: “the face of Imam Husayn”, “The son of Imam Husayn”.

20
Excerpt cuts carry no ellipsis; the 6:75 quote is untagged
PolishBeats 06 · 08 · 10Auditor B

The 2:260 and 28:7 excerpts start/stop mid-ayah with no “…” in Arabic or translation - neither cut distorts meaning (judged honest), but a marker would be transparent and house-consistent with 102:5's trailing “…”. Separately, the beat 06 reflection's quote “that he might be of those of certainty” is accurate 6:75 wording with no “(6:75)” tag (folded into #2's rewrite if approved).

21
“For most, certainty comes only at death” lands as a puzzle
PolishBeat 11 · Act III cardAuditor C

The card asserts the classical reading of its own bridge verse (15:99, yaqin = death) without stating the link. One clause closes it: “For most of us, certainty arrives only at death - when the unseen is finally seen.”

22
The map's first rung states the metaphor as fact
PolishBeat 02b · Depth 1 cardAuditor C

“To know the fire exists - by the smoke on the horizon” is the metaphor's first appearance, unmarked as analogy - and the map's cards are collapsed until tapped, so a reader who opens only one card loses the ladder that teaches it. A two-word simile marker anchors it: “Like knowing a fire exists - by the smoke on the horizon.”

23
“The one who lived to carry it” + unglossed Sahifa
PolishBeat 15 · note + sourceAuditor C

“Carry it” has a fuzzy antecedent (Karbala? the story? the trial?) - “the one who lived to carry its story” fixes it. And al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya is an unglossed book title; two words cover it: “his book of prayers, al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya”. (The note's glossing instinct is otherwise the right one - see clean areas.)

24
“I love not those that set” - archaic inversion
PolishBeat 06 · translationAuditor C

“I love not” plus “those” for celestial objects are mild ESL speed bumps. “I do not love the things that set” keeps the meaning at no cost.

Note that if finding #1 takes fix option (a), Yaqin's beat moves to the Imam's last moments - which also happens to relieve the crowding, though it then abuts Sabr's final-moments climax; the choice between #1's options should weigh this.

Resolution - 2026-07-17

Findings 1-17 applied to YaqinDeepDive.swift, with UR/AR strings mirrored on every changed beat. Finding 1 took option 1b: the summit narration is re-attributed to Imam Ali ibn al-Husayn (Ma'ani al-Akhbar · Bihar al-Anwar), retagged “When the Trial Grew Fierce”, the arrows detail dropped - and the dua note now names the weld (the man who prayed the dua is the man who described that face). Build re-run: green; guardrail greps (em dashes, diacritics, curly ayn marks) clean. Findings 18-26 not applied - recorded here as known and accepted.
