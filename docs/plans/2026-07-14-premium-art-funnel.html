The art is at both ends of the funnel. The middle is where people decide.

The app ships six pieces of art and one video loop. They sit on the first onboarding screen and on the paywall — the moment before anyone knows the app, and the moment after they have already decided. Everything in between, including all fifteen units of premium content, is sold with SF Symbols.

The gap

Fifteen worlds, sold as fifteen strings.

Every immersive Deep Dive and every Inside the Surah experience — the app’s most expensive, most differentiated, most gated content — is represented to the user by an SF Symbol name. Two tiles below show what a cover looks like instead. I generated them for 4 credits while writing this.

The thesis

Nobody converts on a paywall. They convert when they see something they want and cannot have. The paywall only collects.

The app currently builds desire with words and icons, then asks for money over a photograph. The art budget belongs in the middle of the funnel, on the locked surfaces — because a locked text row says “there is more text in here,” and a locked cover says “there is a world in here.”

Ranked by leverage

Three plays.

Play 1

Give the fifteen a face

15 stills · ~30 credits

One cover per gated unit, purpose-composed 4:5 in the existing style bible: emerald-teal night, gold light source, atmospheric haze, no human figures, dark at the top so type stays legible. The eight “Soon” units get covers too — that turns the roadmap into something you are buying into, which is the argument a one-time lifetime purchase actually needs.

Why it converts

A free user cannot evaluate locked content. They can only evaluate what you show them. Right now you show them water.waves.

Play 2

Make the paywall remember what they reached for

1 loop · ~20 credits

There are fourteen paths into the paywall and none of them pass any context. Someone three verses into al-Baqara, someone on day seven of Muharram, and someone reaching for the Comparative tafsir layer all land on the identical screen. The fix is mostly code, and it costs no new art if Play 1 lands: the hero band becomes the cover of the thing they just tried to open.

Why it converts

Desire is specific and it decays. “Unlock the rest of Yusuf” over the well art is a different offer from “Everything. Forever.” over a dome.

Play 3

Let them see through the veil

1 motif · ~4 credits

There is no soft gate anywhere in the app. No blur, no fade, no “read the first passage,” no metering. Content either renders in full or the paywall fires and the content never renders at all. It is a pure binary wall.

Instead: let a locked experience open. Full-bleed cover art, the title, the first beat of the descent readable — then the text sinks into emerald haze and a gold seal. Not a lock icon, which the house style rules out; a seal, or an unlit lantern that lights when you unlock. The art becomes the teaser.

Why it converts

A wall tells you something exists. A veil tells you what you are missing, in your own words, in the register you already trust.

Second tier

Premium feel, and the sessions that feed the funnel.

These do not convert directly. They raise the number of sessions and the sense that the app is worth paying for, which is what every paywall impression is drawn from.

  • Explore and its seven detail screens — zero art, zero motion, seven header slots, one template. Fourteen credits. ExploreView.swift:367–381
  • Today’s hero is a gold gradient rectangle TodayView.swift:863–924. A seasonal art band that changes with the Hijri calendar makes the daily return feel expensive every single day.
  • There is no celebration for a streak, a journey day, or a finished journey. The only one in the app is the badge award. Its confetti and halo machinery BadgeAwardView.swift:349–410 is already written and reusable — and a beautiful completion moment is the thing people screenshot, which is free acquisition.
  • Onboarding has no upsell at all. Thirteen screens end on an account form. The nine bare middle screens plus a final “here is everything inside” wall of the new covers is the most natural first ask in the app.
  • There is no App Store preview video. That is the single highest-leverage conversion asset outside the binary, and it does not exist. The shrine loop plus real screen recordings gets you one.
Boundaries

What Higgsfield should not be used for.

UI micro-interaction

Card transitions, button feedback, reveals. The project has no Lottie or Rive, and should not add one for this. These belong in SwiftUI.

Motion you already have for free

The app renders roughly 150 SF Symbols and uses symbolEffect zero times. matchedGeometryEffect is also unused — a shared-element transition from cover to detail is what will make the new art feel expensive, and it costs no credits.

Dead code, not missing art

IslamicGeometricPattern is 231 lines with no call sites, and darkScreenAura() is a no-op on the roughly 50 emerald screens that call it. Some of the flatness is a wiring problem, not an art problem.

Budget
