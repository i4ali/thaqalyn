1
The response beat's centerpiece sentence fails first-pass reading
Should-fixBeat 11 · He AnswersAuditors A + C

Three problems on the dive's signature screen. (a) The English uses the archaic “No X… but that B” correlative - a first pass can yield the opposite meaning (“no servant takes refuge in Me”), with an aside wedged exactly where the reader holds the structure open, and a nested conditional inside. The hardest sentence shipped in any of the four dives. (b) The gold Arabic shows only the final clause while the italic English quotes the whole hadith - an Arabic-reading user sees a “quote” whose displayed original doesn't correspond (Shukr's response beat aligned the two; the cleaner standard). (c) The reflection's closing metaphor - “the hand that grips creation instead finds the ropes of the heavens cut” - stacks three compressions and misparses as “ropes which cut.”

Current (words)No servant of Mine takes refuge in Me rather than in My creation - I know it from his intention - but that if the heavens and the earth and all within them plotted against him, I would make for him a way out from among them all.
Suggested (words)Whenever a servant of Mine takes refuge in Me and not in My creation - and I know it from his intention - then even if the heavens and the earth and all within them plotted against him, I would make for him a way out from among them all.
Current (reflection close)And the hand that grips creation instead finds the ropes of the heavens cut.
SuggestedAnd whoever grips creation's ropes instead finds that the rope from heaven has been cut.
2
The Dawud promise is never reconciled with the summit
Should-fixBeats 11 → 13Auditor A

Beat 11 stakes Movement III on a concrete promise: “I would make for him a way out from among them all.” Two swipes later the climax says “No sea split that morning; no fire cooled” - and never says what the way out was. A careful reader at the emotional peak can experience the revelation as contradicted by Karbala rather than transfigured by it - the opposite of the design doc's “honest turn.” One clause in beat 12 or 13 closes the loop: the way out ran not through the army but through Him - the promise kept in a different currency, not quietly dropped.

3
“No fire cooled” leans on a story this dive never tells
Should-fixBeat 13 · Climax reflectionAuditors A + C

The sea is earned (beat 08, Musa). The fire is Ibrahim's furnace - Yaqin's material, which Tawakkul's own doc lists under “explicitly avoided.” Inside this dive the most weight-bearing sentence has no antecedent for it: a first-time reader asks “what fire?” The exact allusion class the hardened audit rule bans.

CurrentNo sea split that morning; no fire cooled.
Suggested (in-dive option)No sea split that morning; no rescue came.

Alternative that keeps both images by glossing them: “No sea split that morning, as it had for Musa; no fire turned cool, as it once had for Ibrahim.”

4
The honest turn is spoiled on the threshold map - and cryptic there too
Should-fixBeat 03 · depths item 3Auditors A + C

The summit's one revelation (“His answer is Himself”) is handed out eight screens early on the al-Kifaya card - Yaqin's and Sabr's thresholds describe their summits' state without giving away the twist. And at the map, with no question yet asked, “whose answer is Himself” is also cryptic on first pass. One change fixes both: neutralize the card and let the Movement III divider (beat 10, which needs the turn to frame Dawud correctly) carry it alone.

CurrentTo be carried by the One you trusted - whose answer is Himself.
SuggestedTo be carried by the One you trusted - when every means has ended.
5
The 3:159 reflection quotes a clause the screen does not show
Should-fixBeat 05 · Resolve, Then RelyAuditors A + C

“Consult them, the Prophet is told” - but the displayed excerpt begins at “And when you have resolved,” so the reader re-scans the verse for “consult” and doesn't find it; “them” has no referent; and “the order of the verse” briefly reads as “the command.” The whole claim rests on a sequence whose first step is invisible.

CurrentThe order of the verse is the whole teaching. Consult them, the Prophet is told; then resolve; then rely.
SuggestedThe sequence of the verse is the whole teaching. Consult your companions, the Prophet is told in its opening words; then resolve; then rely.
6
Climax body paraphrase clashes with the displayed translation
Should-fixBeat 13 · bodyAuditors B + C

The translation renders the dua “my trust… my hope”; the body paraphrases the same Arabic as “my confidence and my strength” - and “strength” is also a loose rendering of «عدة» (support/resource). An attentive reader pauses over which is the quote; the payload also arrives late behind an interpolated aside. One rewrite fixes all three.

Current…to name the One who held him: You are, in everything that befalls me, my confidence and my strength.
Suggested…to name the One who held him: in every distress You are my trust, in every hardship my hope.
7
“Tie the camel” is an unglossed hadith allusion
Should-fixBeat 03 · depths item 1Auditors C + B

“Resolve, work, tie the camel” leans entirely on the “tie your camel, then trust” hadith - never quoted or hinted anywhere in the dive; a reader who doesn't know it meets a literal camel at the end of an abstract list. (Auditor B adds: the hadith's classic isnad is Sunni - benign as an idiom since nothing is claimed, but another reason not to lean on it silently.) Gloss it (“tie the camel first, as the Prophet taught”) or replace the clause (“do everything that is in your hands”).

8
Unanswered Prayer: source descriptor is a gloss, and one word tracks a parallel source
Should-fixBeat 06 · source + bodyAuditor B

The narration is genuinely al-Kafi (2:~511, Imam al-Sadiq) but sits in the chapter on those whose supplication is not answered - “on seeking livelihood” reads like a locus and isn't one. And al-Kafi's text has the man say «اللهم ارزقني» (“O God, provide for me”); “O Lord” is the al-Khisal / Qurb al-Isnad wording - the same cross-source detail-bleed class as Shukr's feet-swelled finding, one synonymous word here. Suggested: source → al-Kafi, on those whose prayer is not answered; quote → “O God, provide for me.”

9
Bare “the means” is translationese carrying three screens
Should-fixBeats 03 · 07Auditor C

“Take the means” / “when the means end” / “while the means still work” - English wants an anchor (“the means at hand”, “something you can do”); the bare absolute forces ESL readers to infer a technical sense (asbab) the text never establishes. Anchor it once at first use; e.g. divider: “Trust is proven not while there is still something you can do, but at the moment your means run out.”

10
Movement II: the diptych goes unnamed, and one image is painted twice
Should-fixBeats 07-09Auditor A

Both content beats are verse beats, same era, same antagonist, same move - and the real design (the same tyrant answered twice: once from the shore, once from inside his own palace) is never named, so it reads as sameness. Meanwhile “the sea in front, the army behind” (divider) is immediately repainted as “Pharaoh's army behind, the water ahead” (beat 08). One clause in beat 09's reflection names the pairing (“the same court Musa fled - answered now from within it”); let one screen own the sea-and-army image.

11

Should-fixBeats 14 → 15Auditor A

Release note: “the hands that do not drop what they hold” → next screen, dua note: “the One who does not drop what He holds.” A refrain wants distance; stacked, the second lands as accidental repetition. Keep it in the release (it completes the hands spine); give the dua note a different final clause.
