Sabr - Three Stations of the Patient Heart

Retroactive audit of the second-shipped theme dive, per the theme-deep-dive skill: three flag-only auditors - A (flow, shape & the ledger), B (theology, sourcing & Arabic), C (readability). Blockers

2 findings

Both are cited-book failures on the dive's most sacred beats - the design doc's own verification rows ("Confirm the fuller wording / exact citation") were never closed before shipping. The words themselves are attested in the tradition; the citations are wrong.

1
The climax line is not in al-Luhuf
BlockerBeat 12 · The Last ProstrationAuditors A + B

“صَبْرًا عَلَىٰ قَضَائِكَ يَا رَبِّ، لَا إِلَٰهَ سِوَاكَ” is sourced to al-Luhuf of Ibn Tawus - but two complete al-Luhuf digitizations return zero hits for any fragment of it. Al-Luhuf's actual final-moment words are different (“بسم الله وبالله وعلى ملة رسول الله” as he falls). The line is also absent from all of Bihar al-Anwar, al-Irshad, Khwarazmi's Maqtal, and Waq'at al-Taff. Its traceable carrier is the late maqtal tradition: Maqtal al-Husayn of al-Muqarram p. 283 (20th c.), quoting the popular “Maqtal Abi Mikhnaf” print. Same failure mode the Shukr audit caught - but on the dive's climax.

Fix options: (a) keep the beloved line and recite honestly - Maqtal al-Husayn of al-Muqarram or “the maqtal histories record” (weaker authority, stated plainly); (b) keep al-Luhuf and swap the Arabic to its attested final-moment words. Auditor B's option (c) - the Ashura-morning dua in al-Irshad - is off the table: it is verbatim Tawakkul's climax (“اللهم أنت ثقتي في كل كرب”), verified in TawakkulDeepDive.swift.

2
The closing dua is not in al-Amali of al-Saduq
BlockerBeat 14 · A Prayer in TrialAuditor B

“رَبِّ كَمْ مِنْ نِعْمَةٍ… فَلَمْ يَخْذُلْنِي” is sourced to Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq · al-Amali of al-Saduq - zero hits for every fragment in two complete Amali digitizations (the Amali's al-Sadiq-before-al-Mansur audience, h. 978, contains dialogue only). The real carriers: (a) as Imam al-Sadiq entering upon al-Mansur - Muhaj al-Da'awat of Ibn Tawus, copied in Bihar al-Anwar 91:312-313; (b) near-identical wording as Imam al-Sajjad during Musrif ibn Uqba's march on Medina - al-Irshad of al-Mufid 2:150-151 / Bihar 46:121. The dive's Arabic also mixes recensions: it drops the لك both sources have in clause one (“قَلَّ لَكَ عِنْدَهَا شُكْرِي”).

Fix options: (a) keep Imam al-Sadiq, cite Muhaj al-Da'awat (· Bihar al-Anwar), restore the لك - recommended, because Sabr's is the only non-Sajjad close in the series (Yaqin Dua 20, Tawakkul Dua 28, Shukr Dua 37 are all Imam al-Sajjad; option (b) would make it four Sajjad closes in four dives); or (b) reattribute to Imam al-Sajjad citing al-Irshad, matching that recension - which also changes the intro's “sixth Imam” to “fourth”.

Should-fix

15 findings

Findings 3 and 4 were rated Blocker-grade by Auditor C - the two Karbala-arc beats fail the “surface of the text” bar the later audits were hardened against. Then the Movement II ladder, a factual overshoot at the summit, and the series-ledger gaps.

3
“The Willing Son”: the entire story is off the surface
Should-fixBeat 08 · 37:102Auditors A + C · C rated Blocker

Nothing on the card names Isma'il, Ibrahim, the dream, or the sacrifice. “He said” - who? Commanded to do what? Then “submit to the knife” arrives with no knife ever established. Beats 06 and 09 both name their figures; this one leaves an unnamed boy urging an unnamed man toward an unexplained violent command - the exact al-Duha failure the Shukr post-audit made a mandatory finding.

Current (reflection)The son does not merely submit to the knife - he urges his father toward the command, and names himself patient before the trial has even begun.
SuggestedThis is Isma'il - the son Ibrahim saw himself sacrificing in a dream that was God's command. He does not merely submit to the knife: he urges his father on, and names himself patient before the blade is even raised.
4
“The Last Night”: a scene with no stage
Should-fixBeat 11 · body + reflectionAuditors B + C · C rated Blocker

Four compounding surface gaps: bare “Husayn” is never identified (not as Imam - house style violation, flagged by B - not as the Prophet's ﷺ grandson; his title only arrives one card later); “they want no one but me” has no antecedent - no army, siege, or Karbala is on the card; “lifted his oath from their shoulders” leaves the bay'a unglossed with the pronoun inverted (it is their pledge to him); and the Imam's speech carries no quotation marks, so it reads as narrator voice. The reflection's “knowing the morning” is elliptical on top - knowing what about the morning?

Current (body)On the last night, Husayn gathered those with him and lifted his oath from their shoulders: the darkness is a curtain - take it, and go; they want no one but me. Not one of them left.
Suggested directionOn the night before the battle, with the enemy army camped around them, Imam Husayn gathered his companions and released them from their pledge to him: “The night has covered you - take it as your mount, and go. They want no one but me.” Not one of them left.
5
The Last Night welds al-Luhuf details under an al-Irshad-only citation
Should-fixBeat 11 · sourceAuditor BFix with #4

Al-Irshad 2:91-94 carries the core scene (oath-release, night-as-mount, all refusing, the night of worship in plain wording) - but “like the humming of bees” (لهم دوي كدوي النحل) and “they want no one but me” are al-Luhuf's wording for the same night; Bihar 44:393-394 proves the split (Majlisi marks the bees passage “وقال السيد” = Ibn Tawus, then “رجعنا إلى رواية المفيد”). Same welded-detail class as Shukr's swollen-feet blocker. Fix: widen the source line to al-Irshad of al-Mufid · al-Luhuf of Ibn Tawus (al-Luhuf alone also covers every element).

6
Movement II's ladder wobbles: both rida proof-texts say sabr on screen
Should-fixBeats 08 + 09 vs 07Auditor A

Movement II claims “the station past endurance” - but Isma'il says “among the patient” (الصابرين) and God's verdict on Ayyub is “patient” (صابراً); the word rida appears in no Movement II text. 37:102 survives - its reflection confronts the tension and locates the leap in “do as you are commanded.” 38:44 does not: its rida case rests on “never once resented his Lord,” which is extra-textual (38:41, the verse immediately before, is Ayyub's plea about his affliction). Auditor B judged the reflection internally consistent with the dive's own complaint-to-God doctrine - the problem is the surface anchor, not theology. Direction: rebuild the 38:44 reflection around أوّاب (“ever returning”) as the visible rida marker - returning without demand, praised after the complaint - and drop the “never once resented” assertion.

7
“Rida made visible” - a station regression at the summit junction
Should-fixBeat 11 · reflectionAuditor A

The Movement III divider declares rida complete (“You have learned to accept it.”) and promises the summit - then the very next beat's reflection says “This is rida made visible.” The dive's strongest demonstration of acceptance sits in the movement that claims to be past acceptance, while Movement II (finding 6) had no such demonstration. Direction: reframe the Last Night reflection as the hinge - rida ripening into peace, the night acceptance was finished and stillness began - so the summit movement does not open on the previous station's name.

8
“Bereaved of every son” is factually wrong
Should-fixBeat 12 · bodyAuditors A + B

Imam Ali al-Sajjad - Husayn's son - survived Karbala; in al-Irshad it is he who narrates the last-night khutba from his sickbed. Yaqin's dua note leans on precisely this fact (“the one who lived to carry it”), and today's Yaqin revision made him the summit narrator - so the two dives now assert contradictory facts about the same day. In the series' most solemn beat, the false universal also cheapens the real losses (Ali al-Akbar, the infant).

CurrentBereaved of every son and brother and companion, his body wounded past counting…
SuggestedBereaved of sons and brothers and companions, his body bearing wounds past counting…
9
Ya'qub's beat never says Yusuf is his lost son
Should-fixBeat 06 · 12:86Auditor C

The text never states Yusuf is Ya'qub's son or that he was lost, so the grief's scale is invisible without the backstory; “until the light left his eyes” reads first-pass as poetic rather than the literal blindness of 12:84; “in all those years” points at a separation never established.

CurrentYa'qub wept for Yusuf until the light left his eyes - yet in all those years he laid his grief before no one but God.
SuggestedYa'qub wept for his lost son Yusuf through years of separation, until he went blind from grief - yet in all that time he laid his sorrow before no one but God.
10
The dua beat: stacked archaisms + “the sixth Imam” unconnected
Should-fixBeat 14 · translation + introAuditor CPairs with #2

“How many a blessing You gave me, and how little my thanks” stacks three archaisms (exclamative “how many a”, fronted object, verbless clause) - not first-pass parseable for an ESL reader, while the dua's own second sentence shows the plainer register. And the intro's “the sixth Imam” is never connected to the source line's “Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq” - nothing says they are the same person.

CurrentMy Lord - how many a blessing You gave me, and how little my thanks; how many a trial You tested me with, and how little my patience.
SuggestedMy Lord - You have given me so many blessings, and I gave so little thanks; You have tested me with so many trials, and I showed so little patience.
11
2:156 is a headless clause - and its free “glad tidings to the patient” link goes unclaimed
Should-fixBeat 04 · 2:156Auditors A + C

“Those who, when calamity strikes them, say…” is a subject with no predicate - the verse completes 2:155's “give glad tidings to the patient, who…”. The reader hits the period expecting a main verb that never came, and the one antecedent that literally names the movement's word goes unused - in a dive whose Movement II struggles for surface anchors. Direction: supply the antecedent in the reflection (“the verse before it says whom this describes - give glad tidings to the patient: those who…”) or a bracketed lead-in on the translation, as Yaqin's 2:260 now does.
