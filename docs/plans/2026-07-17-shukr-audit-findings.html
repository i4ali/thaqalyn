Sourcing accuracy is the app's credibility. This is a citation-precision error, not a fabrication - the exchange itself IS in al-Kafi.

1
The “feet swelled” detail is not in the cited al-Kafi hadith
BlockerBeat 12 · The Grateful ServantAuditor B

The al-Kafi narration from Imam al-Baqir has the Prophet standing on the tips of his toes (على أطراف أصابع رجليه) - it does not mention swelling. “Until his feet swelled” is the Bukhari/Muslim wording; within the Shia corpus the swelling appears in a different narration (al-Ihtijaj of al-Tabrisi, from Imam al-Kazim: “stood ten years on the tips of his toes until his feet swelled”). As written, the source line claims something its hadith does not say. The design doc's sourcing row overstates the same point.

Fix options: (a) reword the body to al-Kafi's image - “He stood in the night on the tips of his toes” - keeping the citation; or (b) keep the swelling image and widen the citation to al-Kafi · al-Ihtijaj. Either way, correct the design doc's sourcing table.

Should-fix

10 findings

Four of these sit on the beats a first-time reader must not stumble through: the turn verse, the summit, the hadith of the Grateful Servant, and the closing dua.

2
“This house” resolves to the wrong family - on the dive's turn beat
Should-fixBeat 13 · 76:22Auditors A + C

The reflection never names the Prophet's household, and the nearest prior “house” (beat 11) is Dawud's - so the pivotal turn (“God, who needs nothing, thanks”) rests on a misresolving pronoun plus a garden-path relative clause (“the surah this house was given”). A reader without the tafsir background is lost exactly where the dive pivots. The al-Insan feeding story stays reserved for Ikhlas - this is one identifying clause, not a narrative.

CurrentSpoken in the surah this house was given - to the family who fed the hungry three nights and asked nothing back.
SuggestedSpoken in the surah given to the Prophet's own house - the family who fed the hungry for three nights and asked nothing back.
3
The dua translation is the hardest sentence in the dive
Should-fixBeat 16 · Sahifa Dua 37Auditor C

Three difficulty devices stacked: the inversion “without there settling upon him,” an interrupting aside splitting verb from subject, and the archaic “that which” arriving nine words after its verb. The siblings prove dua register can be formal and linear.

CurrentO God, no one ever reaches an end in thanking You without there settling upon him, from Your goodness, that which binds him to a further thanks.
SuggestedO God, no one ever reaches an end in thanking You - for with every thanks, more of Your goodness settles upon him, and binds him to thank You again.
4
Garden-path quoted question in the Grateful Servant body
Should-fixBeat 12Auditor CPairs with #1

“But you are already forgiven, he was asked - ” reads backwards: the attribution lands after unmarked speech, and the question is split across an aside. Siblings introduce embedded speech with a colon.

CurrentBut you are already forgiven, he was asked - everything before, everything after. Why this?
SuggestedHe was asked: but you are already forgiven - everything past, everything to come - why this?
5
The summit's callback sentence has no stated namer and a late verb
Should-fixBeat 14 · ClimaxAuditor C

A three-item list, a double appositive aside, then the verbless participle “named” - on the beat that must not cost a reread. (Minor secondary: “Anyone can thank for the gift” - the idiom is “give thanks for.”)

CurrentHearing, sight, hearts - the first gifts, the ones this descent began with - named in thanks on the last night they would be his.
SuggestedHearing, sight, hearts - the first gifts, where this descent began. He named them in thanks on the last night they would be his.
6
The “thanks can never be finished” payoff is told three times
Should-fixBeats 02 · 10 · 15Auditor A

Orientation leaveWith → beat 10 reflection → count-beat note. Beat 10 has earned the statement (it is the tongue's limit); the orientation pre-announces it, so the count beat's written resolution reads as repetition instead of discovery. The gesture itself still works.

Current (leaveWith)You'll leave with a map of gratitude - and a prayer for the thanks that can never be finished.
Suggested directionYou'll leave with a map of gratitude - and a prayer that hands the rest back to Him.
7
“Already rendered its thanks” framing undercuts Movements II-III
Should-fixBeat 06 · Thanks of the HeartAuditors A + C

If the heart alone finishes the thanks (“everything the tongue says afterward is overflow”), the tongue and limbs become optional extras - yet 34:13 commands them. Keep the hadith verbatim; reframe the reflection so recognition is where thanks is born, not where it ends. Auditor C separately flags the body's calqued syntax (“Whomever… and he recognizes… - he has…”).

Current (body)Whomever God grants a blessing, said the Imam, and he recognizes it with his heart - he has already rendered its thanks.
Suggested (body)When God grants a servant a blessing, said the Imam, and he recognizes it with his heart - he has already given its thanks.
8
“Five words” does not survive counting
Should-fixBeat 09 · 93:11Auditor A

The displayed ayah is four written Arabic words (وَأَمَّا بِنِعْمَةِ رَبِّكَ فَحَدِّثْ); the English is eleven. The beat invites exactly this kind of close attention. Say “Four words,” or sidestep arithmetic (“A verse the length of a breath”).

9
Mention/use glitch: “was not say thanks but work it”
Should-fixBeat 11 · Movement IIIAuditor C

Unmarked imperatives as noun phrases make “was not say” momentarily ungrammatical. One word fixes the parse without losing the punch: “was not to say thanks but to work it.”

10
The setup to Musa's question is knottier than the answer
Should-fixBeat 10 · He AnswersAuditor C

“Every thanks I thank You with” - a cognate-object relative with a stranded preposition, on the beat's crux. The divine reply below is perfectly clear; the setup should match it.

CurrentTo Musa, who asked: how shall I thank You, when every thanks I thank You with is itself Your gift?
SuggestedTo Musa, who asked: how shall I thank You, when even my thanks is itself Your gift?
