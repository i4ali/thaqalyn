## Station 1 - The Morning After
- **Place/date:** Karbala, 11 Muharram · **Arabic theme:** مَا بَعْدَ عَاشُورَاء · **icon:** `sunrise`
- **Devotional (verified):** *Ziyarat al-Nahiya al-Muqaddasa* - the lament for the martyrs.
  English: *"…as I have been hindered by the course of time and (God's) decree has prevented me
  from helping you… I will lament you morning and evening, and weep blood in place of tears, out
  of my anguish for you."* **Source:** Ziyarat al-Nahiya al-Muqaddasa (attributed to the Imam of
  the Age; recorded by Ibn al-Mashhadi, *al-Mazar al-Kabir*, and in *Bihar al-Anwar*; content
  reliably transmitted, chain "attributed").
- **Verses:** **2:155** (the trial; "give glad tidings to the patient") · **2:156** (the istirjaʿ).
  - *2:155 note:* God tests "with fear and hunger and loss," then commands glad tidings to the
    patient - Karbala is that trial; its survivors are the *sabirin*.
  - *2:156 note:* Their answer to catastrophe - "Indeed we belong to God, and to Him we return" -
    the istirjaʿ spoken over the unburied bodies.
- **Tafsir focus:** By the morning of the eleventh, Ashura's silence had a sound: burning tents,
  the wail of children, the clank of the sick Imam's chains. The bodies lay unburied as the women
  were seated on bare camels and driven away. Passing her brother's body, Zaynab cried out to her
  grandfather: *"This is Husayn, in the open, smeared with blood."* The battle was over; the
  harder work - keeping Karbala from being buried in silence - had begun.
- **Reflection:** We assume the test is the loss itself. The survivors reveal a second test that
  comes after: to carry the truth of what happened when silence would be easier. Their patience is
  not passivity - it is a refusal to let Husayn be forgotten.

## Station 2 - The Road to Kufa
- **Place:** Karbala → Kufa · **Arabic theme:** الطَّرِيقُ إِلَى الكُوفَة · **icon:** `figure.walk`
- **Devotional (verified):** *al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya*, **Supplication 22** (in hardship).
  English: *"O God, I have no endurance for effort, no patience in affliction, no strength to bear
  poverty… entrust me not to Your creatures, but take care of my need alone."* **Source:** al-Sahifa
  al-Sajjadiyya, Duʿaʾ 22 (the Imam who himself walked this road, in chains).
- **Verses:** **3:186** (tested, and "you will hear much hurt") · **2:153** (help in patience & prayer).
  - *3:186 note:* "You will be tested in your wealth and your selves, and you will hear much hurt" -
    the abuse hurled at the captives, met with patience and God-consciousness.
  - *2:153 note:* "Seek help in patience and prayer" - the only provisions the caravan carried.
- **Tafsir focus:** The heads went ahead on spears; the family of the Prophet came behind, unveiled,
  on bare-backed camels, the sick Imam in an iron collar. When a Kufan woman leaned from her window
  and learned who they were, she wept and threw down cloths to cover them. This is the ordeal the
  Qurʾan names - to be tested and to "hear much hurt," and to answer it not with despair but with
  patience and prayer.
- **Reflection:** Bearing witness is rarely dramatic. Sometimes it is only refusing to look away, or
  covering another's shame with your own cloak. The road to Kufa asks what small dignity we can still
  offer when we ourselves have lost everything.

## Station 3 - Kufa: The Voice That Would Not Break
- **Place:** Kufa (Ibn Ziyad's court) · **Arabic theme:** خُطْبَةُ زَيْنَب · **icon:** `quote.bubble`
- **Devotional (PENDING verification):** candidate *al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya*, **Supplication 20
  (Makarim al-Akhlaq)** - an excerpt on truthful, upright speech and God-given dignity. *Gist:* a
  prayer to be helped to speak the truth and to be honored through it - the grace Zaynab embodied.
  **Source:** al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya, Duʿaʾ 20. *(Arabic excerpt to be locked in the final pass.)*
- **Verses:** **16:91** (fulfil the covenant) · **16:92** (do not unravel your yarn - *documented*).
  - *16:91 note:* "Fulfil the covenant of God when you have pledged it, and do not break your oaths" -
    the very pledge Kufa made to Husayn, then broke.
  - *16:92 note:* "Do not be like her who unravels her yarn after it was strong" - the exact image
    Zaynab flung at Kufa in her sermon (confirmed in al-Luhuf, *Balaghat al-Nisa*, *al-Ihtijaj*).
- **Tafsir focus:** In Kufa - the city that had invited Husayn and then abandoned him - the captives
  were paraded before weeping crowds. Then Zaynab raised her hand and "even the camel-bells fell
  still." She turned their tears into a mirror: *"You are like her who unravels her yarn after it was
  strong"* - the image of the Qurʾan itself - people who spun a covenant and then tore it apart.
  Before Ibn Ziyad, asked how she found what God had done, she answered: *"I saw nothing but beauty."*
- **Reflection:** Grief can collapse a person into silence, or be forged into speech that outlives the
  sword. Zaynab had no army and no freedom - only her voice. It was enough to keep Karbala from being
  erased. What truth are we staying silent about?

## Station 4 - The Long Road to Sham
- **Place:** Kufa → Damascus (entered 1 Safar) · **Arabic theme:** الطَّرِيقُ إِلَى الشَّام · **icon:** `sun.dust`
- **Devotional (verified):** *al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya*, **Supplication 7** (in distress).
  English: *"O You by Whom the knots of difficulties are undone, O You by Whom the sharp edge of
  hardships is softened, O You from Whom is besought the escape to the breeze of relief… the
  difficulties have yielded to Your power."* **Source:** al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya, Duʿaʾ 7.
- **Verses:** **2:214** (no Garden without the trial; "God's help is near") · **39:10** (the patient
  rewarded without measure).
  - *2:214 note:* "Do you suppose you will enter the Garden without the trial of those before you? …
    God's help is near" - endurance as the gateway, relief at its very edge.
  - *39:10 note:* "The patient will be paid their reward in full, without measure."
- **Tafsir focus:** From Kufa the captives were driven weeks across the desert, entering Damascus on
  the first of Safar to drums and celebration. The sources spare us a tidy itinerary and give only the
  hardship: heat, exhaustion, humiliation, the sick Imam bound through all of it. The Qurʾan had asked
  whether anyone supposes they will enter the Garden without the trial of those before them - and
  promised, at the edge of endurance, that God's help is near.
- **Reflection:** The long road is its own test - not a single blow but a weariness that will not end.
  Endurance, offered to God, becomes worship. "I have no patience in affliction," the Imam prays - and
  in that honest helplessness casts himself on God alone.

## Station 5 - The Court of Yazid
- **Place:** Damascus · **Arabic theme:** مَجْلِسُ يَزِيد · **icon:** `building.columns`
- **Devotional (PENDING verification):** candidate *Munajat al-Muʿtasimin* ("Whispered Prayer of Those
  Who Hold Fast to God," of the Fifteen Whispered Prayers of Imam al-Sajjad). *Gist:* clinging to God's
  refuge and protection alone - the posture of dignity before a tyrant. **Source:** al-Munajat al-Khamsa
  ʿashar. *(Arabic excerpt to be locked in the final pass.)*
- **Verses:** **3:178** (respite only lets the wrongdoer increase in sin - *documented*) · **14:42**
  (God is not unaware of the wrongdoers - *documented*).
  - *3:178 note:* "Let not the disbelievers think Our respite is good for them; We only give them
    respite to increase in sin" - the verse Zaynab recited to Yazid on his throne.
  - *14:42 note:* "Do not think God unaware of what the wrongdoers do; He only delays them" - her
    warning that his triumph was a reprieve, not approval. (Both are in her documented Sham sermon.)
- **Tafsir focus:** In Yazid's court the family stood bound by a single rope while he struck Husayn's
  lips with a cane and recited pagan verse gloating over the House of the Prophet. Zaynab rose: *"Do
  you think that by taking us captive you have shamed us?"* She recited that the wrongdoer's respite
  only lets him "increase in sin," and that the slain of God are not dead but living. Then the young
  Imam took the pulpit: *"I am the son of Mecca and Mina, the son of Muhammad the Chosen."*
- **Reflection:** Tyranny wants its victims silent and small. The answer of the Ahl al-Bayt was to
  speak - to name themselves before the very throne that tried to erase them. Sovereignty, Zaynab
  reminded the court, is God's to give and to strip. Thrones end; the truth spoken before them does not.

## Station 6 - The Ruin of Damascus
*(renamed from "Prison of Sham": the early sources describe an exposed ruin/khirbah, not a dungeon.)*
- **Place:** Damascus (the captivity) · **Arabic theme:** خَرِبَةُ الشَّام · **icon:** `moon.stars`
- **Devotional (PENDING verification):** candidate *Munajat al-Khaʾifin* or *al-Rajin* ("of the Fearful"
  / "of the Hopeful," Fifteen Whispered Prayers) - grief and hope turned toward God. Fallback: *Munajat
  al-Shakin* (verified, "to You I complain"). *(Final text locked in the last pass.)*
- **Verses:** **12:86** ("I complain of my grief only to God") · **94:5-6** ("with hardship comes ease").
  - *12:86 note:* Yaqub's words - and the posture of the captives' mourning in the ruin of Sham.
  - *94:5-6 note:* "With hardship comes ease; indeed, with hardship comes ease" - the promise doubled.
- **Tafsir focus:** Yazid lodged the family in a ruin exposed to heat and cold, and there they mourned
  Husayn openly. The grief did not stay hidden: as the days passed, the people of Sham began to learn
  who these captives were, and the city's mood turned until Yazid feared for himself. *(A beloved later
  tradition places the death of a small daughter of Husayn here; it is treasured in the mourning
  gatherings, though it is not found in the earliest sources.)* Grief, carried to God, became a summons
  even Damascus could not ignore.
- **Reflection:** There is a sorrow that curdles into despair, and a sorrow that is poured out before
  God and becomes light. The captives' mourning was not weakness; it was the seed that turned a hostile
  city. "I complain of my grief only to God" - and God answered through a watching people.

## Station 7 - The Turn Homeward
- **Place:** Damascus → (via Karbala) → Medina · **Arabic theme:** العَوْدَة · **icon:** `arrow.uturn.left`
- **Devotional (PENDING verification):** candidate *Munajat al-Shakirin* ("Whispered Prayer of the
  Thankful," Fifteen Whispered Prayers) - gratitude for deliverance. **Source:** al-Munajat al-Khamsa
  ʿashar. *(Arabic excerpt to be locked in the final pass.)*
- **Verses:** **21:88** (We delivered him from grief) · **65:3** (whoever relies on God, He suffices him).
  - *21:88 note:* "We answered him and delivered him from grief; thus do We deliver the believers" -
    Yunus's rescue, and the caravan's release.
  - *65:3 note:* "Whoever relies upon God, He is sufficient for him… God will accomplish His purpose" -
    the way out that opened homeward.
- **Tafsir focus:** Fearing the turning mood, Yazid released the family, returned their looted
  belongings, and sent them under escort toward Medina. Reaching Iraq, they asked the guide to lead
  them by way of Karbala. The tyrant who meant to break them now sped them home - and the road bent,
  as it always would, back toward Husayn's grave. *(Tradition relates that the caravan reached the
  grave on Arbaeen itself; scholars differ on the dating, so we hold it gently.)*
- **Reflection:** Deliverance rarely looks like victory in the moment. Here it looked like a weary
  caravan turning, of its own longing, back toward a grave. The covenant was not signed in triumph but
  in return - choosing, again and again, to come back to Husayn.

## Station 8 - Arbaeen: Jabir at the Grave
- **Place/date:** Karbala, 20 Safar · **Arabic theme:** الأَرْبَعِين · **icon:** `hands.sparkles`
- **Devotional (verified):** the **full Ziyarat of Arbaeen** (78 segments), narrated from Imam
  al-Sadiq via Safwan al-Jammal. Opening: *"Peace be upon the intimate servant of God and His most
  beloved… Peace be upon al-Husayn, the wronged, the martyred; peace be upon the captive of agonies
  and the victim of shed tears."* **Source:** *Misbah al-Mutahajjid* (al-Tusi), *Iqbal al-Aʿmal*
  (Ibn Tawus), *Mafatih al-Jinan* (Qummi). *(Full verbatim text in the research file; embedded as-is,
  hamza orthography normalized. — pending your "full vs. excerpt" call.)*
- **Verses:** **3:169** (the slain are alive) · **89:27-30** (O tranquil soul, return to your Lord).
  - *3:169 note:* "Do not think those slain in the way of God dead; they are alive, provided for by
    their Lord" - the ground of the ziyarat: the pilgrim greets a *living* Husayn.
  - *89:27-30 note:* "O tranquil soul, return to your Lord, well-pleased and pleasing" - the Return
    that Arbaeen names. *(Alternative if you prefer the mockup's pick: 2:156.)*
- **Tafsir focus:** On the fortieth day, an old and near-blind companion of the Prophet - Jabir ibn
  Abdullah al-Ansari - came with Atiyya to the grave: the first pilgrim of Husayn. He bathed in the
  Euphrates, perfumed himself, and walked "taking no step but in the remembrance of God," then fell
  upon the grave crying "Ya Husayn!" When Atiyya asked how they could share in the martyrs' reward,
  Jabir answered with the Prophet's words: *"Whoever loves a people is raised with them."* What Jabir
  began that day, millions have never stopped.
- **Reflection:** "Do not think them dead," says the Qurʾan; "they are alive." The Ziyarat of Arbaeen
  is spoken to a Husayn who hears. Loyalty is measured not on the day of the tragedy, but forty days
  later - when the crowds have gone home and only the faithful return. Jabir asks, across the
  centuries: will you still come?
